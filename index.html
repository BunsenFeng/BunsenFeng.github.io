Scaling Participation in Modular AI

Humanity is a mosaic of multifaceted talents and needs, yet the AI models used by all are built by the few: a centralized market of monolithic models, closed or open, is ill-suited to capture the diversity of human knowledge, reasoning, and values.

My research pioneers a new paradigm in which modular AI scales with participation: modular AI systems built bottom-up by diverse stakeholders, who shape the system with their own data, expertise, and priorities. Its foundation is compositional intelligence: intelligence emerges as independently developed modules collaborate, generalizing beyond what any module was built for and growing with new contributions. My research rethinks the AI lifecycle:

  • Train models for modular AI systems: a model's solo performance barely predicts what it adds to the whole, so I train models to strengthen the systems they join (Compositional RL, Knowledge Card, Modular Pluralism).
  • Align models through collaborative feedback: rather than one feedback source, diverse models act as checks and balances, competing, judging one another, and learning from where they disagree (Sparta Alignment, Stackelberg Alignment, Don't Hallucinate, Abstain, Tracking Political Biases).
  • Adapt models via collective search: release is far from a model's ceiling, as models search together through swarm intelligence for better weights and ways to collaborate, and initially weak models grow into the strongest experts (Model Swarms, Heterogeneous Swarms, Data Swarms).
  • Deploy participatory AI at scale: modular AI systems from 61 models built by 440+ researchers at 80+ institutions outperform monolithic models of up to 405B, establishing participation as a new scaling axis; I also safeguard such systems against malicious contributions (Scaling Participation, MoCo, Among Us).

I am also broadly interested in knowledge and factuality, networks and structures, social NLP, multi-agent systems, AI safety, and applications in scientific domains.
